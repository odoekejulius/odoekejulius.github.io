International bestselling books data analysis.

Prepared by Julius Chibuzo Odo-Eke · Report dated November 4, 2026 · 7,909 chart entries, 10 source fields

Explore who appears on national bestseller lists, where the authors come from, and how long books stay, across 45 countries from June 2013 to December 2022.

Coverage: only five countries (Germany, Italy, the United States, Spain and France) have a continuous monthly series, with 5,689 entries. The other 40 countries are snapshots of 1 to 10 months, so every trend uses the five core countries.

Cleaning and validation

The file had no duplicate rows or IDs and no missing dates, countries, ranks or titles. The issues were structural, and no rows were deleted: original values were kept beside the standardised ones.

One finding changed how the results are measured. Italy's list before 2018 is almost entirely Italian authors (97%) and 22% women, then changes character in January 2018, which looks like a source-definition change. Those 550 rows are excluded from every “comparable” measure; left in, they would nearly double the estimated yearly rise in women's share (6.0% against 3.3% in odds).

Questions explored

  • What does the dataset cover, and where is it reliable?
  • Is the share of women authors rising, and do women reach number one?
  • How much of each country's list is domestic versus imported, and is that changing?
  • How long do bestsellers last, and how fast do lists turn over?
  • How concentrated are publishers and authors, and which authors travel?

What the analysis found

  • Gender. Women wrote 43.6% of core entries by single-gender authors, rising from 44.9% in 2013 to 49.8% in 2022 (odds up 3.3% a year, 95% CI 1.012 to 1.054, p = 0.002). The top spot is the exception: women hold 37.2% of #1 positions against 44.3% of ranks 2 to 10 (p = 0.002), but across the top three the gap is not significant (p = 0.28).
  • Home versus imported. 65% of core entries are by authors from the list country. The domestic share is rising in Germany, France and Spain (odds up 15% to 22% a year) but flat in the United States, the most domestic list at 85%. Germany is the most open at 46%.
  • Staying power. 59% of books appear for a single month, the median stay is one month, and 4.5% stay six months or more. The US list churns fastest, with 64% of its entries new that month. Books by women stayed 0.23 months longer on average (2.16 against 1.93 months), though a rank-based test finds no difference in typical stay, so that evidence is mixed.
  • Concentration. The top five publishers hold 49% of entries in Italy, 45% in the United States and 42% in Spain, against 21% in Germany. Publisher concentration indices run from 210 (Germany) to 624 (Italy).
  • Travelling authors. 49 authors charted in three or more core markets and account for 21% of core entries. Five charted in all five: Stephen King, Ken Follett, Dan Brown, Haruki Murakami and Jo Nesbø.

Power BI build package

The project includes Power Query load scripts, DAX measures, a report theme, a setup guide and a validation sheet with 23 expected values. All 23 values were recomputed from the raw data and match. The scripts were checked statically and have not been opened in Power BI Desktop, and no Power BI report file is included. The interactive dashboard on this site is a browser preview of the intended design.

Limitations

  • The lists show top-10 placements only, not sales, and titles are English translations, so the same book can carry different titles in different countries.
  • Author gender and nationality are as supplied and were not verified.
  • The data are observational: associations, not causes. The same book appears in several months, so entries are not independent and the reported intervals and p-values are optimistic. Clustering by book widens the 95% CI for the 3.3% yearly rise in women's share to 0.987 to 1.081.
